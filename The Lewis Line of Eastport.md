# The Lewis Line of Eastport

**Deep-research report — 23 August 2026.**
Jean Lillian Lewis's family, one generation up and sideways; and the verdict on the
Meriwether Lewis story. Produced by an 18-agent research run over the 1910–1950 census
page images, the *Official Register of the United States* 1905–1921, the *Bonners Ferry
Herald* (Chronicling America), the Idaho death index 1911–1951, BC vital registrations,
and the Meriwether Lewis scholarship. Every key claim below survived an adversarial
verification pass in which independent agents re-downloaded and re-read the cited images.

---

## The headline: family memory swapped two women

The c.1911 photo from Colby's trove is captioned `SPENCER - EVA - TOM - DAISY - JEAN,
LEWIS — EASTPORT IDAHO APPROX 1911`, and the family read that as parents Spencer and Eva
with children Tom, Daisy and Jean. Jean's 1976 memorial card says the same. The records
say otherwise:

- **Daisy was the wife — Jean's mother.** The 1910 census (Bonner Co., ED 77, sheet 12A)
  shows Spencer, 34, in his *second* marriage of 1 year 8 months to **Daisy J., 25, born
  Canada, immigrated 1908** — with daughters Eva R. (5) and Jean L. (1). Her obituary
  (*Bonners Ferry Herald*, 29 Jul 1919) says she was born **7 Sep 1885 at Toronto**,
  married Spencer *at Eastport* by Rev. Henderson, and **died at her Eastport home on
  25 July 1919** — Idaho death certificate #026548, the only Lewis death ever registered
  at Eastport. Jean was nine.
- **Eva was a daughter — Jean's older half-sister.** Born c.1905 in Idaho, her mother born
  New York (1910 census) — from Spencer's *first* marriage, c.1904–05. The Herald of June
  1922 calls her "the daughter of U.S. Custom Inspector Spencer Lewis" and reports her
  wedding to **Francis Fowler of Calgary** at Eastport on 26 June 1922, with "Miss Jean
  Lewis" among the shower guests.
- **Tom was younger than Jean, not older** — Thomas R. Lewis, born c.1911 in Idaho, the
  infant in the photo (ages 8 and 18 in the 1920/1930 censuses).
- **There was a fourth child nobody remembered: Della M. Lewis**, born c.1915 in Idaho —
  Jean's full sister, four when their mother died, marked absent from the household at 15
  in 1930, fate unknown.

So the photo shows Spencer, his wife Daisy, and children Eva (~6), Jean (~2) and baby Tom.
A family that lost its mother in 1919 — and then dispersed — garbled its own cast within
two generations. That matters below.

## Spencer A. Lewis, the customs officer

- Born **Michigan, c.1875–76**: four censuses (1910–1940) and *seven* editions of the
  federal *Official Register* (1907–1921, each carrying a state-of-birth column) agree.
  The retirement clipping's "came west out of Michigan" in January 1907 checks out; the
  1905 *Register* has no Spencer Lewis, and his appointment reads "At Large — Idaho", so
  Eastport was his **first federal post**, not a transfer.
- Career: Inspector at Eastport by 1 July 1907 ($3.50/day) → Mounted Inspector (horseback
  patrolman) 1909–1913 → Inspector to retirement, **February 1943** (*Bonners Ferry
  Herald* 18 Feb 1943 p.1 — the family's clipping; *Spokesman-Review* 16 Feb 1943).
  Prohibition-era whiskey seizures, Eastport's WWI Liberty Loan chairman, Secretary of
  the Copper Falls Mining Co. His $800 frame house stood on the border-station land
  itself ("Spencer Lewis' frame building" — Boundary County IDGenWeb).
- **Three marriages**: (1) c.1904–05, to an unidentified New York-born woman (Eva's
  mother) — note Eva's Idaho birth c.1905 implies Spencer was already in the West two
  years before the clipping's arrival date; (2) 1908, Daisy (d. 1919); (3) c.1930,
  **Patricia, born Montana c.1911** — 35 years his junior, two years younger than Jean —
  with five children born 1931–38: Anna Mae, Adele Rae, Spencer Jr., Wayne Roger, and
  Phillip Gene. Jean had five half-siblings younger than her own son.
- In the **1930 census, Jean appears in her father's Eastport house as "Robison, Jean L.",
  20, with her infant son "Robison, Herbert S., grandson, born Canada"** — Bert, before
  the family settled into the CPR towns.
- After retirement the family left Boundary County (no Lewis household anywhere in the
  1950 Eastport enumeration); Spencer was still alive and visiting in **July 1959**. His
  death has not been found: he is in neither the Idaho index (to 1951) nor the BC index
  (to 2005). Washington — where three of Patricia's children were born and where the
  candidate Tom settled — is the leading guess.

**Where the paternal chain ends:** at Spencer himself. The 1920 and 1930 censuses both
record his **father born OHIO, mother born NEW YORK** — but in 1910 he answered "Don't
Know" to both, a hint he may have been orphaned or estranged young. No names, no 1900
Michigan Soundex card, no public tree, no published claim.

## Daisy's probable maiden identity — new this run

The Herald's Eastport columns of 2 Aug and 13 Sep 1912 both say: *"Mr. and Mrs. J. W.
Spence of Cranbrook, B.C. spent Sunday and Monday at the home of Mrs. Spence's sister,
Mrs. Spencer Lewis."* The only adult Spence in Cranbrook in the period is J. **H.**
Spence (Wrigley's 1918 BC Directory — "J. W." is a typo), and the original BC marriage
registration (1908-09-162863, image read) shows John Henry Spence marrying **Annie
Patrick Macdonald** — born **Dalkeith, Scotland**, daughter of **William MacDonald and
Alexanderina Patrick** — at Cranbrook, 28 Apr 1908, witness Mary Macdonald.

If "sister" is literal, **Daisy was née Macdonald**, one of at least three daughters
(Annie, Mary, Daisy) of a Dalkeith family that emigrated to Ontario by 1885. This is a
well-supported hypothesis, not proof; it also explains a conflict — censuses and Jean's
1976 death registration variously call Daisy "Canada-French"/born Quebec, but her
household's mother tongue was English and her obituary says Toronto. Her death
certificate (#026548, orderable from the Idaho State Archives) would settle it and name
her parents.

## The Meriwether Lewis story: verdict

**Impossible as stated; unsupported in any looser form.**

- Meriwether Lewis (1774–1809) **never married and had no children** — his estate went
  to his mother; even the 2000s campaign to exhume him was led by collateral kin (a
  great-great-great-great *nephew*).
- His only full brother **Reuben died childless** ("They had no children" — Monticello's
  Lucy Marks exhibit). His only full sister **Jane's nine children were Andersons** (one,
  Dr. Meriwether Lewis Anderson, inherited Locust Hill in 1837). His half-siblings were
  **Markses**. The Lewis surname died out in that household with Meriwether and Reuben.
- Therefore **no one surnamed Lewis can have Meriwether as a "great uncle" of any
  degree** — a great-uncle is a direct ancestor's brother, and no sibling of Meriwether
  has Lewis-surnamed descendants. The only loosely-true shape would be descent from one
  of his paternal **uncles** (John, Nicholas of The Farm, Charles, or Robert of Granville
  — sons of Robert Lewis of Belvoir), which would make Meriwether a first cousin three or
  four times removed — the kind of kinship families round up to "some kind of great uncle."
- Against that possibility: Spencer's line ends at an unnamed Ohio-born father; no
  record, tree, or certified-descendant archive links any Michigan or Idaho Lewis family
  to the Virginia clan; Lewis was the 29th-commonest surname in America; and unfounded
  Meriwether-descent lore is a documented recurring phenomenon (a famous, childless name
  is a magnet). And this family's oral memory demonstrably swapped Jean's own mother for
  her half-sister at one generation's depth — five-generation lore transmitted through it
  carries little weight on its own.
- **What could still change the verdict:** naming Spencer's parents (1880 Michigan
  census; Daisy's and Spencer's certificates) and tracing the Ohio-born father's line
  back toward or away from Virginia — or, decisively, a **Y-DNA test** on a male-line
  descendant of Spencer (Thomas R.'s line, or Spencer Jr./Wayne/Phillip's) against the
  documented Warner Hall Lewis haplotype.

## The record-ordering shortlist

1. **Idaho death certificate #026548** — Daisy Lewis, 25 Jul 1919, Boundary Co. Names
   her parents; settles Macdonald and Toronto-vs-Quebec. (Idaho State Archives, or free
   in FamilySearch's *Idaho Death Certificates 1911–1937* with a login.)
2. **Bonner County marriage record**, Spencer Lewis × Daisy, c.Aug–Sep 1908 (Sandpoint
   courthouse; FamilySearch *Idaho County Marriages*).
3. **1880 Michigan census** search (indexed, needs FamilySearch/Ancestry login): a
   household with son Spencer b. c.1875–76, father b. Ohio, mother b. New York.
4. **WWI draft card**, Sept 1918, Boundary Co. — Spencer's exact birth date; and his
   **federal personnel/retirement file** at NARA St. Louis (retired 1943).
5. **Washington Digital Archives** death indexes + FindAGrave by hand — Spencer's death
   and grave; also the Marysville WA Tom Lewis (d. after 2003).
6. Paywalled but located: *Bonners Ferry Herald* 18 Feb 1943 p.1 (the retirement article
   = the family clipping) and **14 Apr 1927 p.1 — Jean's wedding announcement**,
   corroborating the typed chart's 12 Apr 1927 date.

*Loose end, deliberately not grafted on: a Paradise Valley Cemetery transcription reads
"Minnie Viola Lewis, 1887–1912, Husband: Spencer Lewis" — but Mrs. Spencer Lewis (Daisy)
was alive at Eastport that autumn, so it is likely a different Spencer Lewis or a
transcription error. Check the original before believing it.*
