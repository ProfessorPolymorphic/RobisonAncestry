# The Golden–Field–Nakusp Robison Lineage

## Overview

The available evidence supports a six-generation paternal line from **County Tyrone, Ireland**, through **New Brunswick**, **Prince Edward County, Ontario**, and **Carman, Manitoba**, to the Robison family associated with **Field, Golden, Nakusp, and the Arrow Lakes region of British Columbia**.

One connection remains formally unproved: whether Bill Robison’s father, recorded as **“H. Barrie Robison,”** was **Herbert Barringer Robison**, born in Carman in 1904. The identification is highly persuasive, but it should remain marked as probable until a marriage, birth, death, railway, church, or family record explicitly confirms it.

## Reconstructed Family Tree

```text
Hugh Robison
born 1766, County Tyrone, Ireland

│
└── Samuel Robison
    married Jane Arthur
    │
    └── William A. Robison
        married Mary E. Barringer / Berringer
        │
        └── Herbert Edward Robison
            1869–1933
            married Anna Falconer, 1882–1933
            │
            └── Herbert Barringer Robison
                born 1904
                probably the “H. Barrie Robison” in Bill’s obituary
                married Jean [maiden name not yet established]
                │
                ├── Herbert Spencer “Bert” Robison
                │   approximately 1928–2013
                │
                └── William James “Bill” Robison
                    1938–2011
```

The first four generations are supported by a 1904 Prince Edward County family history and an independently compiled Manitoba Historical Society biography drawing on vital registrations, newspapers, burial records, and other sources. The connection from Herbert Barringer Robison to “H. Barrie Robison” is the one probable link that still requires direct documentary confirmation.

## 1. The Immigrant Generation: Hugh Robison and Rebecca Dougall

The earliest identified direct paternal ancestor is **Hugh Robison**, described in a 1904 county history as having been born in **County Tyrone, Ireland, in 1766**.

Before leaving Ireland, Hugh married **Rebecca Dougall**, daughter of **Dr. William Dougall**, a physician in the same county. Hugh and Rebecca emigrated to **New Brunswick around 1792**, bringing their infant first son, also named Hugh. After about fourteen years in New Brunswick, the growing family moved to **Hillier Township, Prince Edward County, Upper Canada**, around 1806 or 1807.

Hugh purchased approximately 200 acres at Stinson’s Block and developed what became known as **Robison’s Point**. The account describes him as Presbyterian and says he died at age 76 and was buried on the old family property. Rebecca’s father later also came to Prince Edward County and practised medicine in Picton.

Because this account was published more than a century after Hugh’s birth, its earliest dates should be treated as strong inherited family history rather than as the equivalent of an original Irish baptismal record. It is nevertheless unusually specific about the family, migration route, land, relatives, and descendants.

## 2. The Prince Edward County Generations

Hugh and Rebecca’s son **Samuel Robison** married **Jane Arthur** and remained in Hillier Township. Their children included **William A. Robison**.

William A. married **Mary E. Barringer**, spelled “Berringer” in the 1904 county history, and settled in **Consecon**, also in Prince Edward County. Their children included a son named Herbert. Most importantly, the 1904 account states that this Herbert, son of William A. of Consecon, had become a prominent lawyer in **Carman, Manitoba**.

That statement provides the key bridge between the Ontario family and the Manitoba family.

## 3. Herbert Edward Robison: Ontario to Manitoba

The Manitoba Historical Society identifies the Carman lawyer as **Herbert Edward Robison**, born at Consecon on **March 19, 1869**, the son of **William A. Robison and Mary E. Barringer**.

Herbert Edward taught school before studying law and moved to Carman in 1899. He served as mayor of Carman from 1911 through 1913 and was appointed King’s Counsel in 1914. He married **Anna Falconer** on July 11, 1900.

Their five recorded children were:

- Ivan Campbell Robison, born 1901
- William James Robison, born 1902
- **Herbert Barringer Robison, born 1904**
- Norah Kathleen Robison, born 1905
- William Falconer Robison, born 1908

Herbert Edward died in Carman on July 18, 1933.

This generation is especially well supported because the Manitoba account cites birth registrations, contemporary newspaper reports, burial transcriptions, and biographical sources.

## 4. Why Herbert Barringer Was Probably “H. Barrie”

Bill Robison’s obituary states that he was the son of **H. Barrie and Jean Robison** and that he was born in Golden on August 1, 1938.

The identification of H. Barrie as Herbert Barringer is supported by several intersecting facts:

1. **The name fits.** “Barrie” is a plausible shortened form of “Barringer,” while the obituary preserves the first initial “H.”
2. **The place fits.** Herbert Barringer was born into the established Carman Robison family, and Bert Robison’s obituary says Bert was born in Carman.
3. **The chronology fits.** Herbert Barringer, born in 1904, would have been approximately 23 or 24 when Bert was born around 1928.
4. **The family naming pattern fits.** His British Columbia sons were Herbert Spencer and William James; Herbert Barringer himself had brothers named William James and William Falconer.
5. **The migration fits.** Bert was born in Carman, while Bill was later born in Golden.

Taken together, these points make the identification a **high-confidence working conclusion**. What is still missing is a direct record stating that Herbert Barringer Robison was the husband of Jean and father of Herbert Spencer and William James.

## 5. Manitoba to British Columbia

Herbert Spencer “Bert” Robison’s obituary says he was born in **Carman, Manitoba**, grew up mainly in **Field, British Columbia**, and began working for Canadian Pacific as a teenage railroad telegrapher. He spent 43 years with CP Rail and retired in 1986 as a customer-service supervisor. He lived his final ten years in Golden and died there on August 25, 2013, at age 85.

William James “Bill” Robison was born in **Golden on August 1, 1938**. His obituary identifies his parents as H. Barrie and Jean and identifies Bert as his brother. Bill later became a teacher, coach, and principal in School District 10 and devoted substantial unpaid time to tutoring children in Nakusp.

This produces a reasonably tight migration window:

```text
County Tyrone, Ireland
        ↓ about 1792
New Brunswick
        ↓ about 1806–1807
Hillier and Consecon, Prince Edward County, Ontario
        ↓ Herbert Edward moved in 1899
Carman, Manitoba
        ↓ sometime between approximately 1928 and 1938
Field and Golden, British Columbia
        ↓
Golden and Nakusp family branches
```

The family’s move into British Columbia therefore occurred after Bert’s birth in Carman and before Bill’s birth in Golden, with the family establishing itself in the Canadian Pacific railway communities of Field and Golden.

## Ancestral Background

The safest conclusion is that the paternal Robison line has a documented **County Tyrone origin**. Hugh’s Presbyterian affiliation is consistent with a possible **Ulster-Scots or Scots-Irish background**, but this does not by itself prove that his ancestors came from Scotland. The evidence currently takes the line to County Tyrone, not yet to a specific Scottish parish.

Additional surnames entering the direct ancestral line include:

- Dougall
- Arthur
- Barringer / Berringer
- Falconer
- Jean’s presently unknown maiden surname

These maternal branches may eventually lead into Scottish, English, Irish, German, or other ancestry, but surname origins alone are not sufficient evidence. Each branch needs its own documentary trail.

## Living British Columbia Descendants and Privacy

Public obituaries establish substantial descendant branches through Bill and Bert Robison. They do not establish where every living descendant resides as of 2026.

The responsible conclusion is that the family has documented long-standing connections with:

- Field
- Golden
- Nakusp
- the Arrow Lakes region

This lineage should not automatically be applied to every person named Robison in British Columbia. Other unrelated, or only distantly related, Robison families have also lived in the province.

No attempt was made here to identify or publish private residential information about living relatives.

## Surname Variants

Historical records may use several spellings for the same family. Searches should include:

- Robison
- Robinson
- Robertson
- transcription or indexing errors involving those forms

The spelling variation is important because census records, land records, church entries, and later transcriptions may not preserve the family’s preferred spelling consistently.

## Research Gaps

The most important unresolved questions are:

1. What was **Jean Robison’s maiden name**?
2. Does a marriage registration identify **Herbert Barringer Robison and Jean** as a couple?
3. Does Bert’s birth registration name his parents?
4. Does Bill’s British Columbia birth registration name his parents in full?
5. Did H. Barrie work for the Canadian Pacific Railway, and did that employment cause the move to Field or Golden?
6. Can Hugh Robison or Rebecca Dougall be located in original County Tyrone church, land, tax, or estate records?
7. Can the Robison line be traced from County Tyrone to an earlier Scottish or English origin?
8. Can the Arthur, Barringer, Falconer, and Dougall branches be documented independently?

## Best Next Records to Obtain

The most decisive next document would be a Manitoba marriage registration for **Herbert B. or Herbert Barringer Robison and Jean**, likely dating from approximately 1925–1928.

Other high-value records include:

- Bert Robison’s Manitoba birth registration
- the 1931 Canadian census household containing Herbert, Jean, and Bert
- a British Columbia birth registration or delayed registration for Bill Robison
- H. Barrie Robison’s death registration or obituary
- Canadian Pacific Railway personnel or pension records
- church baptism and marriage records from Carman, Field, or Golden
- family Bibles, funeral cards, certificates, letters, and labeled photographs

A record giving **Jean’s maiden name** would open half of the ancestry at the Bill-and-Bert generation and may be the single most valuable family clue presently available.
